# DubbingAI Storyboard v2

## Intent
- This version is intentionally more demo-animation driven than the previous storyboard.
- It should feel like a living system on screen, not a sequence of static cards.
- Motion is used to explain how Lucius works inside a busy Discord support environment.
- The sequence should still remain readable without voiceover.

## Global Motion Rules
- Every scene needs one primary motion idea that can be described in a single sentence.
- Supporting motion should keep the frame alive without competing with the focal path.
- Every scene needs a readable hold before transition out.
- No flicker, no blank flashes, no generic HUD overlays, no decorative chaos.
- Motion should favor threads, queues, clustering, consolidation, and handoff logic over abstract AI magic.

---

## Scene 1 - Case setup

### Purpose
- Establish the customer, the platform, and the scale of the support surface.

### Static frame
- Left 45%: editorial headline block with `DubbingAI`, `30K-member Discord`, and one short setup sentence.
- Right 55%: a clean Discord-like field with several conversation threads already visible at different depths.
- Background should feel calm and premium, not noisy.
- The Lucius mark should not dominate here; this is still the customer's problem space.

### On-screen objects
- Headline block
- small status pills
- 4 to 6 Discord thread strips
- subtle grid / dot texture behind the thread field

### Entering state
- Scene opens on a mostly quiet frame.
- Headline is already faintly present.
- Thread field is mostly empty, with only one anchor thread visible.

### Motion spine
- A quiet Discord field becomes visibly active as more threads enter and settle into place.

### Primary motion event
- Message threads slide in from staggered offsets and settle into a structured cluster on the right.

### Secondary supporting motion
- Tiny unread indicators or line pulses wake up as the threads arrive.
- Background dot-grid drifts very subtly, just enough to keep the frame alive.

### Hold state
- The viewer should get one clear read:
  - this is DubbingAI
  - this is Discord
  - this is already a large support surface

### Exit
- The thread field continues moving, but unresolved density starts to build.
- The right side becomes slightly more crowded just before cut.

### Viewer takeaway
- This is a real customer support case inside a large Discord community.

### Transition to next scene
- Use the same thread objects and increase density rather than replacing the scene with a hard cut.

## Scene 2 - Human overload

### Purpose
- Show that scale becomes a support burden when the same human team keeps absorbing repeated work.

### Static frame
- Left side: a stack of repeated user questions and support requests.
- Right side: a small human team strip or mod lane with only three visible operators.
- Bottom or lower corner: a subtle always-on pressure indicator.
- Layout should feel tighter and more compressed than Scene 1.

### On-screen objects
- repeated question cards
- escalation flags
- human mod avatars or labels
- pending queue rail

### Entering state
- Carry over the thread field from Scene 1.
- Some of the threads are now unresolved and stacked.

### Motion spine
- Repeated support requests keep stacking faster than the human side can absorb them.

### Primary motion event
- Question cards stack upward and forward in staggered waves, visibly outpacing the human response lane.

### Secondary supporting motion
- The three human-mod markers light up one by one, then stay pinned while more questions continue arriving.
- Escalation tags appear on a few cards and remain visible.
- One or two cards can briefly nudge toward the human lane and stop there, to imply interruption cost.

### Hold state
- The frame should clearly communicate:
  - repeated questions
  - overloaded queue
  - very small human team

### Exit
- The queue freezes in a too-much-incoming-work state.
- Motion pauses for a beat before Lucius enters.

### Viewer takeaway
- The problem is operational overload, not community growth by itself.

### Transition to next scene
- Queue cards stay in the scene and become the inputs that Lucius starts reading.

## Scene 3 - Lucius enters the workflow

### Purpose
- Show Lucius as part of the support system, not a layer floating above it.

### Static frame
- Three-column system frame:
  - left: live inputs and repeated questions
  - center: Lucius node
  - right: structured outputs such as repeated intent, likely resolution, escalation needed
- The center should feel stable and authoritative, but not oversized.

### On-screen objects
- Lucius mark / node
- incoming signal chips
- structured output notes
- a few connector paths or flow lines

### Entering state
- Queue density from Scene 2 remains.
- Lucius node is not fully visible at first.

### Motion spine
- Noisy support inputs begin to resolve into structured support logic around Lucius.

### Primary motion event
- Repeated question cards collapse into signal chips that travel inward toward the Lucius node, then re-emerge on the right as structured notes.

### Secondary supporting motion
- Connector lines draw in after the main chip movement, not before.
- Right-side output notes appear in short stagger, each locking into a fixed vertical rhythm.
- A few input chips should continue drifting in while the outputs settle, so the system feels live.

### Hold state
- Lucius is now visibly positioned inside the workflow.
- Inputs on the left and outputs on the right are both readable.

### Exit
- The right side stabilizes into a more systematic arrangement.
- The structured notes become the setup for the next learning-loop explanation.

### Viewer takeaway
- Lucius learns from actual community behavior and turns noisy support activity into reusable structure.

### Transition to next scene
- The structured outputs on the right expand or fan out into the four-part system loop.

## Scene 4 - Learning loop

### Purpose
- Explain why Lucius improves instead of behaving like a frozen rules bot.

### Static frame
- Four linked cards or modules arranged in one readable system:
  - onboard from history
  - learn in the flow
  - optimize over time
  - escalate edge cases
- The layout can be 2x2 or a compact horizontal loop, but each step must remain scan-friendly.

### On-screen objects
- four system cards
- directional connectors or step relationships
- one short explainer line near the top or left

### Entering state
- Cards are not all present at once.
- One anchor card appears first, then the rest assemble into the loop.

### Motion spine
- A static explanation becomes a living process as the loop assembles and starts passing activity through its steps.

### Primary motion event
- The four cards assemble into a loop from dispersed positions, then one signal token travels through them in order.

### Secondary supporting motion
- Each card subtly wakes up as the token reaches it.
- Connectors brighten only while active, then return to a resting state.
- The loop should cycle once, not endlessly and noisily.

### Hold state
- After one complete cycle, the loop remains fully assembled and readable.

### Exit
- The loop's token exits toward the results scene.
- The cards stay present for a beat, then fade behind the metrics build.

### Viewer takeaway
- Lucius keeps improving by combining history, live learning, optimization, and human handoff.

### Transition to next scene
- The final loop token becomes the first metric build trigger.

## Scene 5 - Results

### Purpose
- Land the hard outcomes without turning the scene into a flashy KPI celebration.

### Static frame
- Top or left: one results statement.
- Main field: five metrics arranged as a strong, balanced grid.
- Metrics that must appear:
  - `30K Members`
  - `80% Automation`
  - `15 days`
  - `3 -> 0 Human Mods`
  - `24/7 Coverage`

### On-screen objects
- results headline
- metric tiles
- faint structured background field

### Entering state
- Scene enters from the learning loop's structured system, not from empty white.

### Motion spine
- The operating model resolves into measurable outcomes.

### Primary motion event
- Metric tiles rise or lock into place one by one with restrained stagger, as if the system has made them legible.

### Secondary supporting motion
- The `3 -> 0` tile can animate as a reduction rather than a simple pop-in.
- The `80%` tile can use a partial fill or ring settle, but keep it understated.
- Other tiles should use variation in timing, not gimmicks.

### Hold state
- All five metrics must be on screen long enough to read cleanly.
- Nothing else should compete with the numbers during the hold.

### Exit
- The metrics soften in dominance while one human quote anchor starts to emerge.

### Viewer takeaway
- Lucius materially changed the operating model for DubbingAI.

### Transition to next scene
- Use the metric field as the background that the human-outcome scene grows out of, rather than fully wiping it away.

## Scene 6 - Human outcome

### Purpose
- Convert the result from operational efficiency into human relief.

### Static frame
- Left: the holiday-relief quote in a large editorial treatment.
- Right: a calm support panel showing that support is still running.
- The scene should feel more spacious and lighter than Scene 2.

### On-screen objects
- quote
- calm status panel
- support flow rows
- holiday marker or relief tag

### Entering state
- Metrics from Scene 5 recede into the background.
- The human quote begins faintly.

### Motion spine
- Operational pressure gives way to calm continuity.

### Primary motion event
- The overloaded system language dissolves into a calmer operating panel while the quote settles into focus.

### Secondary supporting motion
- The support panel rows can pulse or tick softly to show that the system is still working.
- The holiday marker should appear late, as the emotional payoff, not first.
- Keep motion slow and breathable here.

### Hold state
- Quote and calm support panel both remain readable together.
- This should feel like relief, not triumph.

### Exit
- The right-side support panel reduces into a minimal structure.
- The quote fades more gently than the operational UI.

### Viewer takeaway
- The outcome is not only automation. It is real relief for the team while support still keeps running.

### Transition to next scene
- Reduce the busy support geometry into a simpler, more foundational community structure.

## Scene 7 - Method ending

### Purpose
- End on the broader principle the case demonstrates, not a sales CTA.

### Static frame
- Left or foreground: a minimal channel structure such as `general`, `help`, `showcase`, `announcements`.
- Right or opposite side: the final principle line in editorial serif treatment.
- Background should feel warmer, more settled, and less operational than prior scenes.

### On-screen objects
- minimal channel bars
- dot-field or structural texture
- final principle statement

### Entering state
- Support geometry from Scene 6 is still slightly visible, then simplifies.

### Motion spine
- Busy support activity resolves into a stable social structure that can sustain participation.

### Primary motion event
- Dense support geometry collapses and simplifies into four stable channel bars, while the final principle line resolves in.

### Secondary supporting motion
- Dot-field lightly reorders itself as the structure simplifies.
- Channel bars can settle one by one rather than appearing all at once.
- Final headline should enter with confidence but not drama.

### Hold state
- This scene needs the longest clean read in the sequence.
- The viewer should have time to absorb the principle without another new motion event stealing attention.

### Exit
- No hard CTA.
- Hold on the principle and let the scene end in stability.

### Viewer takeaway
- Lucius does not just reduce tickets. It helps create the conditions for a living community that can sustain participation.

### Transition to next scene
- End of sequence.

## Sequence Rhythm Notes
- Scene 1 to Scene 3 should feel increasingly active and system-oriented.
- Scene 4 is the explanatory center, but it still needs a clear demo-style movement cycle.
- Scene 5 is the strongest data lock.
- Scene 6 is the emotional decompression scene.
- Scene 7 is the method landing and should breathe the most.

## Motion Vocabulary Notes
- Prefer accumulation, clustering, consolidation, routing, loop traversal, reduction, and settling.
- Avoid overusing simple fade-ins as the default answer.
- Avoid making every scene use the same card-rise motion.
- Preserve at least one stable anchor in each scene so the motion always has something to play against.
